Breathing, read from the ECG

07 · RESPIRATION
How it works {{ tip('edr') }}With each breath the lungs push the heart around a little, so the R-peak height on the chest ECG rises and falls with breathing. Counting those waves gives breaths per minute.
Cross-checks {{ tip('rsa') }}The heart also speeds up while you breathe in (RSA), and the strap accelerometer feels the chest move. At rest, all three are compared.
Limit while rowing {{ tip('coupling') }}Each stroke also moves the electrodes at the stroke rate. Breathing usually locks to the stroke, so the two can't be separated: the report shows the lock ratio instead.

Why rowing breathing is uncertain: 32 s from the middle of the longest piece. The chest motion (dashed) is almost pure stroke. Here {{ '%.0f' % (figs.spectrum_share * 100) }}% of the ECG breathing signal's power (filled) sits on the stroke frequency or its multiples, where breathing and electrode motion can't be told apart. The rest (the other peaks) could be breathing that is not locked to the stroke, or noise. The paced-breathing calibration and, later, the neural-net model are how we'll find out.

{% endif %}
Method & validation

R-peaks: an HR-guided detector (8–25 Hz energy, adaptive threshold) + Kubios artefact correction. EDR = R-peak amplitude, cleaned, band-passed to 5–72 /min, resampled at 4 Hz. Rate = spectral peak over 32-s windows every 5 s, kept only at rest and only when the peak holds > 25 % of the band power. Agreement at rest: EDR vs ACC {{ '%.0f' % (summary.breath_agree_edr_acc * 100) if summary.breath_agree_edr_acc is not none else '–' }}%, EDR vs RSA {{ '%.0f' % (summary.breath_agree_edr_rsa * 100) if summary.breath_agree_edr_rsa is not none else '–' }}%. Next step: a neural-net EDR model scored against a paced-breathing block at the start of each session.

Aerobic threshold from HRV

08 · DFA α1
What α1 is {{ tip('alpha1') }}A measure of how "organised" the tiny beat-to-beat variations are. Relaxed heart: complex, fractal rhythm (α1 ≈ 1). Hard effort: the rhythm turns random (α1 → 0.5).
Why it matters {{ tip('vt1') }}The crossing of α1 = 0.75 lands close to the lab-measured aerobic threshold (VT1). That is the ceiling for your easy days, measured from your own heart and not from an age formula.
How to read the chartsBlue band = below threshold (good for base training). Stone band = grey zone. Red = above the anaerobic threshold. ✕ = window rejected because the ECG was too noisy to trust.
